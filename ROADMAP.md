# Roadmap

This is the plan for finishing the project. Task-level CAD records live in
[docs/CAD_TASKS.csv](docs/CAD_TASKS.csv); older sprint history is in
[docs/SPRINT_PROGRESS.md](docs/SPRINT_PROGRESS.md) and
[docs/REVIEW_READY.md](docs/REVIEW_READY.md).

## Finish line

The owner adopted public-data-first investigation, with physical validation
later. Finish on two separate tracks:

1. Reproduce command-response identification from real driving data, compare
   with a simple baseline, and evaluate held-out whole runs. Freeze selection,
   the whole-run split, preprocessing, metrics and any justified use tolerances
   before final-test evaluation. Report estimates without an invented pass
   threshold. Tire-parameter claims require adequate signals, excitation and
   known vehicle properties plus an identifiability check.
2. Resolve low-mount packaging and the D2 scope decision from required scan
   planes, obstructions and pitch/roll geometry before fabrication. Then qualify
   and measure the selected assembly response if physical work continues.

A planar LiDAR measures intersections with its scan plane. The delegated
assessment starts with the lowest rigid mount that preserves the required scan
field. Final height and design remain unapproved. If the owner ends mast work,
close it as a design study. A raised assembly requires qualified measurements in
both bending directions, repeated taps and remounts. Racing suitability also
requires excitation and allowed scan error.

The existing vehicle simulations and mast results remain historical studies.
Simulator identification checks consistency with data generated by the same
model. Keep those results reproducible.

## Where it stands

- Public driving data: the [executed development comparison](reports/real_command_response.md)
  inventories a licensed bag and compares command-response fits on that run.
  Its [candidate review](runs/real_command_response/candidates.json) supports
  retaining command-response scope. The [split qualification](reports/driving_split_qualification.md)
  found that published metadata does not identify acquisition sessions or parent
  segments. No separate final set was admitted; other runs remain unopened.
- Historical vehicle software: see the [integrated report](reports/final_report.md).
- Mast design: the original frequency was a
  [hand calculation](runs/mast_hand_calc/summary.txt). The redesign has
  [hand calculations](runs/mast_hand_calc/design_sweep.txt) and
  [FEA output](runs/mast_fea/fea_summary.txt).
- The [tip-mass attachment study](runs/mast_modal_attachment_20260925/study.json)
  explains much of the hand/FEA gap. Its coupled, centred model remains
  unconverged; the historical model and results are preserved.
- The [drivetrain assessment](docs/design/16_mechanical_design_analysis.md#61-retrospective-assessment--is-the-200-hz-guard-the-right-criterion-audit-f1)
  shows shaft order crossing the predicted mast mode within the operating
  speed range. The historical frequency threshold is under review because
  it does not establish motor-vibration clearance. The crossing alone does
  not establish harmful vibration; the excitation amplitude and response
  remain unknown.
- CAD: tube, support sleeve and root clamp are parametric SOLIDWORKS parts.
  The assembly was accepted against an independent CadQuery model
  ([#55](https://github.com/500ft/autonomous-racing-systems/pull/55)).
- Test tooling exists for static compliance, load-cell calibration and bench
  logging, with synthetic tests. There are no measurements of the owner's assembly.
