1. CORRECT THE DECISION, BEFORE CHOOSING AN INSTRUMENT
===================================================

The supplied plan is directionally right that R² is binding in these particular
assumed scenarios. It overstates what that result establishes in four ways.

A. A passing median is not campaign reliability.

Read-only reproduction used current committed fixture_feasibility.py and its
actual imported campaign estimator, loaded in memory without editing source.
Nominal inputs were retained; only repeatability_mm changed. Each trial was a
single-axis synthetic campaign of 30 points. Seed 20260925, 5,000 trials/case:

  Assumed repeatability    Median R²     Fraction R² >= .99    Relative U95
  0.5 micrometres          .986027       .1270                 .04510
  0.3 micrometres          .992519       .8602                 .03288

Monte Carlo standard errors of these pass fractions are about .00471 and
.00490. They describe simulation sampling precision, NOT confidence that the
assumed physical model is correct. These are single-axis R² pass fractions,
not probabilities of satisfying every gate on both axes.

At the original seed 20260923 and 400 trials, the same read-only calculation
gave median R² .986064/.992660 and relative U95 .04606/.03422 respectively.
Thus the quoted 4.3% maximum needs its exact input set, trial count, source
revision and seed. It is not reproduced as a bound by this current setup.
Do not dismiss the difference as a bug without comparing those inputs.

Do not turn 0.3 micrometres into a purchase specification or a newly frozen
acceptance threshold. It is one favourable assumed operating point with a
substantial simulated rejection fraction. To estimate a two-axis result, draw
shared calibration/environment errors once per campaign and independent channel
errors at the appropriate scope. Multiplying independent per-axis probabilities
would assume independence that the physical setup may not have.

B. Resolution is not repeatability.

The statement that a 1 micrometre indicator cannot have 0.3 micrometre reading
repeatability is too categorical. JCGM defines resolution and repeatability
separately [R1,R2]. A display can return the same rounded value repeatedly while
concealing substantial error. Conversely, fine resolution does not prove low
drift, hysteresis or uncertainty. Under a uniform quantization model, a 1
micrometre step contributes 1/sqrt(12) = .289 micrometres standard uncertainty;
this is a model contribution, not a manufacturer's repeatability specification.

In this screen repeatability is added BEFORE rounding. Specify whether a
supplier/bench repeatability value includes quantization before entering it;
otherwise the same effect can be counted twice. Evaluate the complete installed
chain: displacement sensor, mount, contact force, alignment, acquisition, drift,
range, calibration and root correction. LVDT/capacitive are candidates, not
automatically adequate or the only viable technologies.

C. Low scatter does not establish low total error.

screen_axis reports relative_U95 from slope scatter and separately reports
relative_bias. A fixed load-dependent root-rotation error changes the slope and
may leave an excellent R². The overall gate does not currently reject excessive
bias. Zero uncorrected_root_rotation_mm_per_n is an explicit ideal assumption,
not measured evidence. Do not describe the scatter-only result as a complete
uncertainty budget or certify a physical chain from it.

D. The existing ranking answers a narrower question than W3.2 needs.

rank_terms sorts uncertainty with one term active at a time. Quantization stays
active through cfg even when its reported term is zeroed. A deterministic
rotation bias can rank low because the metric is scatter. The ranking is not
an additive variance decomposition and does not rank R² rejection risk.
Rank feasible interventions using changes in gate risk, bias and uncertainty,
with effort/access recorded, rather than copying dominant_terms[0].
