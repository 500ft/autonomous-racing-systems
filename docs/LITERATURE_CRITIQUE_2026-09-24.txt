Research question and scope
===========================

Which published measurement and structural-dynamics methods materially change
the reliability of this project's force calibration, fitted static compliance,
and nominal mast modal reference, given its purchased electronics and actual code?

This is a targeted audit of the existing literature synthesis and its application,
not a second exhaustive bibliography or a verification of all 126 literature rows.
Vehicle control literature was not independently re-reviewed in this pass.

Eligibility: a source must directly address calibration/model adequacy, correlated
uncertainty, conformity decisions, structural joints or transducer mass loading.
Relevance grades: 3=core, 2=relevant, 1=background, 0=excluded. Citation counts and
publication prestige did not determine eligibility or task priority.

Evidence types are separate: M=mathematical/methodological work, G=metrology
guidance or normative definitions, B=one controlled experimental study. G and M
are not grades of replicated experimental validation. Repo observations and
synthetic counterexamples are identified separately; neither is hardware evidence.

1. Repair the calibration verdict before using its uncertainty output
====================================================================

Observation, verified by synthetic execution:
  Starting from known_affine.json, add
      20000 * reference_mass_kg**2
  to every point's counts_mean; leave the other fields unchanged.
  analyse() returns:
      fit_quality.verdict = LACK_OF_FIT_SIGNIFICANT
      lack_of_fit_F       = 622532.7721752349
      verdict             = CALIBRATION_USABLE
      blockers            = []

In a separate run, remove zero_start_counts, zero_end_counts and u_g_m_s2 from
the original fixture. The result is again CALIBRATION_USABLE, with missing zero
readings reported but no unresolved term. The gravity-uncertainty default is zero.

This is a decision-logic defect, independent of which fitting method is preferred.
The tool computes diagnostics but some do not participate in its final verdict.
The lack_of_fit function also uses a fixed F=5 cutoff and calls the result
statistically significant/non-significant. NIST's classical test instead uses
the significance level and numerator/denominator degrees of freedom [S1].

Claude work order P0-A
  Files: experiments/load_cell_calibration.py, its existing tests and protocol.
  a. Make one explicit list of required evidence and model-adequacy conditions.
     A failed condition prevents CALIBRATION_USABLE; an unevaluable required
     condition gives CALIBRATION_INCOMPLETE, with a specific reason.
  b. Make zero observations and uncertainty terms mandatory or explicitly bounded
     with a stated basis. Distinguish a justified negligible term from missing data.
  c. Replace the arbitrary significance wording. For a classical replicated,
     fixed-x, homoscedastic model, use the appropriate F distribution and declared
     alpha. For the present heteroscedastic errors-in-variables model, establish
     a compatible test or simulation-based null distribution; merely importing
     an F quantile does not make that model satisfy the classical assumptions.
  d. Retain residual plots versus force, time and direction and a held-out check.
     Separate up/down behavior from pure repeatability. Do not pool a systematic
     direction effect into noise and then call the relationship adequate.
  e. Require an explicit held-out role or separate validation input; the current
     analyse() fits all supplied points. Preserve a way to generate diagnostic
     conversions from incomplete data, but label them unqualified and keep them
     out of accepted downstream calibration inputs.
  f. Treat CALIBRATION_USABLE as a defined, tested claim about its declared scope;
     it cannot substitute for campaign readiness or an independent review.

Acceptance examples:
  - The curved fixture above cannot return CALIBRATION_USABLE.
  - Missing zeros or an unresolved required uncertainty cannot return usable.
  - A perfect synthetic affine fixture has a defined zero-residual result;
    0/0 in a lack-of-fit ratio must not become an unexplained failure or pass.
  - Held-out observations do not alter fitted coefficients.
  - Missing repeats, inconsistent direction labels, nonfinite data, and invalid
    reference/count uncertainties produce named refusals, not numeric exceptions.

Support: S1, with S5 defining additional load-cell error mechanisms.
Confidence: high for the reproduced logic failures. The best replacement test
depends on the adopted error model; it is not settled by citing an F-test paper.

2. Make uncertainty represent the whole measurement, including shared errors
=========================================================================

Published basis: GUM explicitly requires relevant input correlations [S2]; its
Monte Carlo supplement propagates distributions and provides interval/stability
procedures [S3]. EA-4/02 ties coverage to the output distribution and effective
degrees of freedom, not simply the number of regression rows [S4].

Observed in E3:
  - u_force includes g uncertainty separately in every row, but the fit assumes
    independent point errors. One gravity value, one reference calibration and
    repeated use of the same mass can introduce shared errors across rows.
  - counts_sd/sqrt(n) assumes independent readings without a correlation check.
  - force_uncertainty combines coefficient covariance and one scatter term; it
    does not explicitly include residual creep, temperature, mounting and
    direction effects, or demonstrate that the scatter term bounds them.
  - coverage_factor returns 1.96 for every degree of freedom above 30, while
    claiming Student-t 95% coverage. It changes from 2.042 at 30 to 1.96 at 31.
    The fit's n-2 is then used for the combined budget without an effective-DOF
    calculation. Exact 95% and EA's approximately 95.45% convention must also
    be distinguished instead of borrowing one source's label for the other.

Observed in W3.1:
  - Fresh independent Gaussian draws represent nearly every error at every row.
  - True force and measured force are independently perturbed around a nominal
    target, rather than measuring the same latent applied force.
  - Indicator quantization is represented as Gaussian noise of matching SD.
  - Reference-model uncertainty and several shared calibration terms are absent.
  - U95 is 1.96 times the simulated slope SD; simulation count defaults to 400,
    without an interval-stability check or a reported estimator-bias diagnostic.

Claude work order P0-B1
  a. Define a measurement equation and error inventory before changing sampling.
     Separate common calibration/reference quantities, cycle drift and individual
     readout variation. Record distributions, units, dependence and uncertainty
     sources; shared quantities are drawn once at their physical scope.
  b. In each synthetic trial, create a single true applied force per observation.
     Generate force readings from that force plus calibration/readout errors and
     displacement from that same force plus its physical/measurement errors.
  c. Carry E3's coefficient covariance into the whole campaign. Sample shared
     reference-mass and gravity errors consistently; avoid counting them twice.
  d. Model quantization as rounding of the underlying signal, or justify another
     model from actual readout noise. Use effective sample size or block analysis
     when ADC/averaging correlation makes n raw samples less than n independent
     observations. Do not mandate an elaborate drift tool before collecting data.
  e. Report bias and interval endpoints as well as SD. Increase Monte Carlo
     batches until endpoints stabilize to a declared reporting tolerance; if a
     compute cap is reached, report numerical precision unresolved.
  f. Use a justified effective-DOF method or direct propagated coverage interval.
     Do not assume all Type B terms have infinite reliability or n-2 governs the
     whole budget. Separate force-channel uncertainty, compliance uncertainty and
     uncertainty in the model-comparison decision.

Acceptance: repeated samples reduce the independent-noise contribution but do
not erase a common gain error; shared-input changes move all affected records
coherently; interval results remain stable when the trial count increases; missing
reference uncertainty prevents a complete comparison claim.

These are project-specific implementation requirements inferred from S2-S5 and
the inspected measurement model, not a claim that those sources prescribe this
software architecture. Confidence: high on the omissions; their numerical impact
on real hardware is unknown until input records exist.

3. Enforce fixture gates and model root rotation as physics
=========================================================

Verified synthetic reproduction on the W3.1 branch:
  run(NOMINAL with both fixture stiffnesses replaced, trials=120, seed=20260924)

  Declared stiffness    Required stiffness    Overall verdict
  1 N/mm per axis       7759.8366 N/mm        PLAUSIBLE_PENDING_PHYSICAL_INPUTS
  8000 N/mm per axis    7759.8366 N/mm        PLAUSIBLE_PENDING_PHYSICAL_INPUTS

Both cases return the same x-axis relative U95=0.0428036412. Both report the
x-axis R² gate false. Declared stiffness is checked for presence but its magnitude
does not control the final verdict; the R² flag also does not control that verdict.
The default UNKNOWN behavior is useful, but filling fields is not acceptance.

Claude work order P0-B2
  a. Enforce positive dimensions/stiffness, required stiffness on EACH axis,
     resolution/count conditions and every gate the overall verdict claims to
     cover. Publish a gate-status map: pass/fail/unknown/not_evaluated.
  b. Either evaluate a gate or make the report explicitly partial. Do not label
     the complete fixture plausible when a required gate fails or is unevaluated.
  c. Keep zero fixture motion and perfect stiffness out of defaults. Account for
     support motion using the actual measurement geometry and correction model.

Independent calculation illustrating the root-motion issue:
  Frozen FEA compliance C = 0.176/128.76 = 0.001366884... mm/N.
  Suppose uncorrected root rotation adds 0.005 mm at 20 N, proportional to force.
  Observed compliance = C + 0.005/20 mm/N.
  Slope bias = 18.2898%, while R² = 1.000 for the noiseless five-level curve.

This constructed example does not prove actual root rotation. It proves that
excellent linearity and repeats cannot detect or average away this kind of bias.
Modelling it only as independent zero-mean read noise misses that failure mode.

The static-to-modal bridge also needs an identifiability check. Under a simple
linear cantilever model, with transverse force and displacement both at height L:
  delta/F = L^3/(3EI) + 1/K_translation + L^2/K_rotation.
Units must be consistent; rotational stiffness has units of moment/radian.
Five force levels at one geometry estimate one combined compliance. They do not
independently identify EI, translational restraint and rotational restraint.
Additional root observations, independent stiffness information, or a deliberately
different geometry/load-height experiment are needed to separate those terms.
Any new physical matrix remains a prospective protocol proposal.

Acceptance: 1 N/mm fails; one bad axis fails; missing terms remain unknown; the
linear root-rotation example shows its bias; an underdetermined stiffness fit
reports NON_IDENTIFIABLE rather than returning a uniquely established parameter.

Support: S2-S3 for measurement propagation; equations and numerical example are
independent mechanics/arithmetic in this review. S8 supports considering joint
state and excitation dependence, but supplies no stiffness value for this clamp.
Confidence: high for the reproduced gates and identifiability calculation.

4. Audit the modal model before explaining its 13.5% frequency gap
================================================================

The literature claim M2 says the 330.1-to-285.5 Hz gap is expected physics rather
than a modelling defect. The listed mechanisms are hypotheses, not an attribution.

Actual code at the inspected main revision:
  experiments/mast_fea.py, build_mesh:
    tip_center_node = min(tip_pool, key=lambda t: hypot(t[1], t[2]))[0]
  write_modal_inp:
    one MASS element is attached to that existing node.
    NROOT translations are fixed.

The tube is hollow. The closest existing tip-plane material node lies on/near
the inner wall, not the empty cross-section center. No center reference node or
tip-ring coupling is created by that selection. The root is explicitly fixed,
so physical clamp flexibility cannot explain a gap between THESE two numerical
models. Sensor-body rotary inertia/extent are also not explicitly represented
by the one-node mass definition. Their relevance to future hardware does not
establish them as causes of this computed difference.

Claude work order P1-A
  a. Export the selected mass-node coordinates, radial offset, total added mass,
     root constraints and the first several mode shapes from a fresh model.
     Confirm which mode is global bending; do not identify it by index alone.
  b. Define the intended ideal reference: a centered tip mass with a physically
     stated attachment/coupling. Compare it with the existing single-wall-node
     attachment while keeping all other inputs fixed. A rigid tip coupling is
     itself a modelling assumption, not automatically the correct physical joint.
  c. Compare equivalent analytical/beam and solid models. Then add shear effects,
     distributed payload/rotary inertia, and root flexibility ONE AT A TIME where
     appropriate. Record effect sizes instead of assigning causes by analogy.
  d. Check symmetry, mode shapes, mass participation where available and mesh
     sensitivity for each declared observable. Preserve the old 285.5 Hz result
     with its original provenance; do not overwrite it as if it were a rerun.
  e. Revise claim M2 now to: the cause of the discrepancy is unresolved; model
     equivalence and the mass connection require verification. Report the old
     number as the output of its recorded model, not a verified physical target.

Acceptance: source and report agree on actual mass location/connection; equivalent
models are compared; causes are attributed only after controlled variations.
Do not widen the existing ±15% band to conceal a changed result.

Confidence: high that the source attaches mass as described and fixes the root;
unquantified effect on frequency. No fresh mesh or solve was run in this review.
Support: source inspection; S7 establishes that mass location can matter but does
not quantify this distinct eccentric-attachment case.

5. Correct the literature synthesis where its conclusions outrun its sources
=========================================================================

Claude work order P1-B: edit literature/claim-ledger.md and affected domain files.

  - "OLS is wrong whenever both axes have uncertainty": qualify this. Cantrell
    compares regimes where bivariate fitting matters and where simpler fitting
    is satisfactory [S6]. Classical attenuation assumes a particular error model;
    shared gain errors and controlled-load uncertainty need their own analysis.
    Keep the frozen campaign estimator for compatibility until a prospective
    amendment is reviewed. Compare candidate estimators on predeclared cases.
  - "Lack-of-fit requires a new physical test matrix": the current protocol
    already has repeated load/unload cycles at each force. First assess whether
    those provide valid within-condition replicates; up/down and drift may need
    separate treatment. Additional repeats are conditional, not automatic [S1].
  - "Five levels means five observations and n-2 coverage": distinguish unique
    levels, independent cycles, correlated ADC readings and effective DOF [S4].
  - "The thin-wall approximation makes the annular I suspect": the inspected
    section-properties function uses pi*(OD^4-ID^4)/64, the exact annulus formula.
    Beam kinematics, shear and boundary assumptions still merit checks, but that
    is a different limitation from approximating the cross-section's I.
  - "Rayleigh automatically brackets this FEA": verify the trial field, mass
    model and boundary conditions are the SAME problem before invoking an upper
    bound. A rounded effective-mass coefficient is not a demonstrated tight bound.
  - "Measured not assumed" in the E3 protocol's expected count-noise discussion:
    replace with SYNTHETIC SENSITIVITY RESULT. Its fixtures are explicitly synthetic.
  - "No governing standard exists": write "none was located in this scoped
    search" unless an exhaustive basis exists. A catalogue entry verifies a
    document's identity/scope, not unread clauses or proof of this apparatus.
  - Do not classify an adopted standard, analytical formula and replicated
    experiment as one interchangeable strength of empirical evidence. Keep
    source access, method applicability and physical validation separate.

Acceptance: each changed claim has source, applicable assumptions, evidence type,
counter-evidence and confidence; no hardware conclusion is inferred from a title,
synthetic fixture, generic mechanism or numerical fit alone.

6. Improve future modal testing and acceptance without adding hardware now
========================================================================

S7 reports location-dependent accelerometer mass loading. That supports a placement
tradeoff, not a universal near-root prescription. Near a fixed root the bending
response also becomes small. Choose location from expected mode response, measured
noise, added mass/cable effects and the quantity being identified. Weigh the actual
board plus mounting/cable contribution; do not reuse an illustrative 5–10 g as a
measured LIS3DH assembly mass. Confidence: moderate for the placement recommendation
until this project's mode shapes and noise are available.

S8 shows preload and excitation can change bolted-joint response in its test rig.
Use that as a reason to record joint state and eventually check amplitude
dependence, not as proof the present split clamp slips or has a known damping ratio.

Keep E4 exploratory. The existing NAU7802 force channel does not support a roughly
285 Hz input-force FRF. Separate acceleration-only decay/peak work from a calibrated
input-output modal experiment; choose the method and decision rule before buying
or building a different force sensor.

Keep the 200 Hz design guard, but do not describe it as a literature-proven LiDAR
perception requirement. A future application study should connect measured base
excitation and mast motion to sensor-pose/scan error and a downstream metric.
This is proposed validation work, not a reason to change the numerical guard today.

S9 treats conformity as a decision with uncertainty and an explicit rule. Prepare
a prospective rule for the ±15% model comparison: specify the observable,
uncertainty of the difference/ratio, shared terms, and handling of a boundary-
overlapping interval. A guarded acceptance rule is an option, not a universal
mandate or permission to reinterpret past results. Keep compliance U95 separate
from uncertainty in the reference-model comparison.

Limits and evidence strength
============================

The strongest findings here are source-level defects reproduced with synthetic
counterexamples and metrology guidance whose applicability is direct. The two
structural experiments support mechanisms, not this mast's frequency, joint
stiffness, sensor noise or calibration capability. The exact cause and magnitude
of the modal discrepancy remain unresolved. No real calibration or physical
validation is established by this review. Publisher records consulted showed no
retraction notice for the selected papers; a comprehensive corrections/retraction
audit of the existing 126-entry bibliography was not performed. Paywalled clause
content and search snippets alone were not treated as verified requirements.
