# 02 — Interview defence

Two rules first, then the drill.

**Rule 1: lead with the limit.** Every strong claim in this repo has a boundary, and stating it yourself
converts a vulnerability into evidence of judgement. An interviewer who discovers a limitation you hid
stops trusting everything else you said. One who hears you name it first starts trusting the rest.

**Rule 2: know which kind of number you are being asked about.** Requirement, assumption, simulation
output, or measurement. Most awkward questions are really "do you know what you actually have?"

---

## The opener, when they say "tell me about this project"

> "It's a system-identification and control pipeline on a 1/10-scale autonomous race car, plus a
> mechanical design package for the LiDAR mast. The technically interesting part is the evidence
> discipline: every number carries whether it's a requirement, an assumption, a simulation output, or a
> measurement — and in the mechanical lane almost nothing is a measurement yet. The checkers refuse to
> report a pass while an input is missing. I'd rather show you a result I can bound than one I can't."

That invites the good follow-up — "what can't you bound?" — which you are prepared for.

---

## The three questions you must not fumble

### Q: "You report 100 % variance-accounted-for. Is that real?"

**Answer it before they finish asking.**

> "It's real arithmetic and a meaningless validation. The data were generated by the same model structure
> the estimator fits, so there's no model error and no noise — the parameters are exactly present in the
> data. Recovering them to 1e-10 proves the optimizer and the plumbing are right. It says nothing about a
> real tire. Fitting a model to data from that same structure validates the estimator, not the model.
> The result I'd actually defend is that the pipeline is correct; the sim-to-real claim is unmade, and
> closing it needs real telemetry, which has been done on scaled platforms in the literature."

**Why this works:** the number looks like an overclaim until you show you know exactly why it isn't one.

**Follow-up you should expect:** *"So how would you validate it properly?"* — Real excitation data from a
physical car, or at minimum a deliberately mismatched plant. Published work identifies tire parameters on
a real scaled racer from about 30 seconds of on-track data; that is the comparison to beat.

### Q: "Pure pursuit beat MPC? That seems wrong."

> "On a nominal plant with no disturbance and no active constraints, there's little for the optimizer to
> buy. The geometric law is already near the achievable error, and MPC is minimising a cost I chose, not
> the metric being scored — so it can be optimal for its own objective and second on mine. MPC's real
> value here is explicit constraint handling and a bounded solve time, 1.33 ms at p95, and that's all I
> claim for it.
>
> I'll go further: I couldn't find a published result in the same direction, and there is one on real
> F1TENTH hardware at 11 m/s where a model-aware pursuit controller beats plain pure pursuit by about 4×
> on lateral error. So my result is scoped to a nominal plant and a modest speed regime, and the next
> test is a mismatched-plant sweep — wheelbase, mass, friction, command delay — because theory says a
> certainty-equivalent optimal controller degrades under model error while a simpler law may not."

**Why this works:** you volunteer the contradicting evidence. That is the single most credible thing a
candidate can do.

### Q: "Is the mast validated?"

> "No. Nothing mechanical has been measured. It's a coherent design study on largely assumed inputs:
> hand calculation, FEA, a mesh study, a tolerance stack, and a frozen test protocol that hasn't been
> run. The fixture doesn't exist, so the feasibility screen's honest verdict is UNKNOWN — it refuses
> rather than guessing a stiffness. Fifteen of thirty register rows are assumptions or pending."

Never soften this. The repo's whole value is that it doesn't.

## Technical depth questions

### On the section and stiffness

**"Why 20 mm and 1.5 mm wall?"** It came out of a design sweep over length, diameter, wall and material
against two criteria simultaneously: clear the modal guard and keep the crash-case yield safety factor
acceptable. The first geometry, 120 mm × 16 mm, passed strength at safety factor 4.05 but failed the modal
guard at 174.7 Hz. Shortening and fattening the tube raised `I` by 2.07× and `k` by 3.58×.

**"Did you use the thin-wall approximation for I?"** No — the exact annulus, `π/64(OD⁴ − ID⁴)`. The
thin-wall form would have been 25 % high here. *(Knowing which approximation you avoided is a strong
answer.)*

**"Where does 3EI/L³ come from?"** Derive it: integrate `EI·y″ = −F(L−x)` twice with zero displacement
and zero slope at the root. See [01 §2](01-engineering-derivations.md).

### On the frequency gap — your best story

**"Your hand calc says 330 Hz and your FEA says 285. Which is right?"**

> "Neither, as originally explained — and finding that out is the part I'd want to tell you about.
>
> I'd attributed the 13.5 % gap to shear, rotary inertia, tip-mass extent and root flexibility. Then I
> audited the actual solver deck. Both models fix the root, so root flexibility can't explain a
> difference between them — neither contains it. And the deck carries a one-node point mass, so tip
> rotary inertia isn't in it either. Two of my four mechanisms were impossible.
>
> Worse, the tube is hollow, so there's no node on the axis: the mass had been attached to the nearest
> node, 8.5 mm off-axis on the inner wall, uncoupled. So I ran three cases on one mesh changing only the
> attachment. The attachment model was worth +10.97 % and the off-axis position only +1.27 %. The
> attachment was the dominant artifact, about nine times the eccentricity.
>
> With it corrected the model sits 2.8 % from the hand calculation instead of 13.5 %. I did **not** adopt
> the new number as a headline, because a new coupling invalidates the old mesh-convergence evidence."

**Follow-up: "How did you know the fix was right?"** An independent check from a different direction: the
orthogonal bending pair splits by 15.8 Hz with the off-axis mass and 0.004 Hz when centred. A centred mass
on an axisymmetric tube *must* give a degenerate pair. The frequencies alone couldn't tell me that.

**Follow-up: "Why three cases and not two?"** Because comparing the original against the fully corrected
model changes two things at once — attachment *and* position — and gives +12.4 % that you'd be tempted to
call "the eccentricity effect". One factor at a time, or you learn nothing.

### On the modal guard — the finding that criticises your own design driver

**"Why 200 Hz?"**

> "Two halves. Clear the 100 Hz control rate by 2×, which is a real requirement. And clear 'a plausible
> low-hundreds-Hz motor band' — which, I found on audit, cites no frequency anywhere in the repo.
>
> The drivetrain numbers were already there. Motor speed at 10 m/s is 20 600 rpm, so first-order shaft
> frequency is 343 Hz. That's *above* the 285 Hz mode, not below it, and it sweeps up through the
> resonance at about 8.3 m/s — inside the operating envelope. So the criterion assumed the band sat below
> the mode and had to be cleared upward, and the drivetrain doesn't behave that way.
>
> That doesn't mean the mast is inadequate. Whether a swept resonance matters depends on unbalance, the
> transmission path and damping, none of which I have. It bounds frequencies, not amplitudes. What it
> establishes is that the criterion's justification is missing — and this threshold drove the whole
> redesign, so it's the most consequential unverified input in the lane."

**Why this is a strong answer:** you audited your own design driver and reported against your own
interest. That is what senior engineers do.

### On the measurement

**"What makes this test hard?"** Five micrometres. Full scale is 27 µm and the bottom of the range is
5.15 µm, so quantization at a 1 µm step contributes 0.289 µm per reading and the differenced pair 0.41 µm.

**"What's the failure mode you're most worried about?"** Root rotation.

> "A load-proportional root rotation adds a constant to the *slope*, and a line plus a line is still a
> line. I simulated it: 0.00025 mm/N of uncorrected rotation gives a 19.4 % slope error while median R² is
> 0.9947 and relative U95 is 3.3 %. Both frozen numerical gates pass and the answer is 19 % wrong. No
> amount of repetition detects it and no fit statistic can. That's why it's an observability requirement
> on the apparatus — two spaced root stations — rather than something you fix in post."

**"Why does the fixture need to be 10× stiffer?"** Series springs: the fixture's compliance adds to the
specimen's, so at `n` times stiffer it contributes `1/n` of the error before correction. And note what the
ratio does *not* constrain — rotation.

**"Why not just average more readings?"** Because it only helps per-reading errors. A calibration gain
error is shared across the whole campaign, scales every force identically, and therefore scales the fitted
slope; `1/√N` does nothing to it. That is also why the two axes aren't independent — one calibration
serves both — so you can't multiply per-axis pass probabilities.

**"Why is R² ≥ 0.99 a weak gate?"** Because a value near 1 is compatible with visible curvature, and
because at 0.3 µm assumed repeatability the median passes while about one campaign in seven is rejected. A
passing median is not reliability. *(And note the replacement isn't just another p-value — non-rejection
doesn't prove adequacy when power is low.)*

## Questions about process, which senior interviewers ask

**"How do you know your own numbers are right?"** Fail-closed checkers plus provenance. A release refuses
while any input is pending. A calibration verdict requires every listed condition to pass — and an earlier
version of that tool returned "usable" while its own lack-of-fit check said the model was inadequate,
which is exactly the class of bug the conditions map now prevents. There are also guards that fail if a
document and the source it describes drift apart.

**"What's the worst mistake you made?"** Pick the modal attribution. It is a real error, you found it, you
fixed it properly with a controlled experiment, and you reported it against your own interest. Second
choice: computing a diagnostic and then not gating on it — a screen once called a 1 N/mm fixture
"plausible" against a 7760 N/mm requirement because the declared value was displayed but never compared.

**"What would you do differently?"** Gate on every computed check from the start. Both of the worst bugs
were the same shape: a number was calculated, displayed, and then ignored by the verdict.

**"What's left?"** The experimental half. Every remaining blocker is an owner input or a physical
measurement: interface drawings, calibration records, a fixture, indicators, a reviewer. Four of ten CAD
tasks are done and five are blocked; ten of fourteen sprint tasks are done and four are blocked.

## Traps to avoid

| Don't say | Say instead |
|---|---|
| "The model is validated" | "The pipeline is verified; validation needs real data" |
| "100 % accuracy" | "Exact recovery of the simulator's own parameters — an estimator check" |
| "The mast is good to 285 Hz" | "The model of the mast predicts 285 Hz; nothing has been measured" |
| "MPC underperformed" | "MPC was second on tracking, which is expected on a nominal plant; its case is constraints and runtime" |
| "FEA confirms the hand calc" | "They agree within the declared band — two models agreeing, not a measurement" |
| "The tolerance stack passes" | "It passes with a mandated levelling step, and its largest term assumes a process we since rejected" |
