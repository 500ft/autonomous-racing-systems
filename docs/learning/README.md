## The 60-second version

> I built a system-identification and control pipeline on a 1/10-scale autonomous race car, and a
> mechanical design package for the LiDAR mast that sits on it. The interesting part is not that it
> works — it is that the repo is built so you can tell exactly how much each claim is worth. Every
> number carries its provenance: whether it is a requirement, an assumption, a simulation output, or a
> measurement. Almost nothing in the mechanical lane is a measurement yet, and the repo says so.

That last sentence is the one that lands. Most student projects overclaim; this one is engineered not to.

## The 5-minute version

There are **two lanes**, and keeping them apart is the point.

**The vehicle lane** is simulation and control. A single-track vehicle model, cornering stiffnesses
identified by bounded nonlinear least squares from simulator telemetry, an EKF study, an FMEA, and three
path-tracking controllers compared on one track with fixed seeds. Two honest results come out of it:

- The identification recovers the simulator's own parameters to about 5 × 10⁻¹⁰ relative error, with
  100 % variance-accounted-for on a held-out rollout. **That is a statement about the estimator, not
  about a real car.** Fitting a model to data generated by that same model structure validates the
  pipeline and nothing else.
- Tuned **pure pursuit had the lowest cross-track error** (0.158 m RMS), below MPC (0.169 m) and LQR
  (0.179 m). MPC's justification is constraint handling and a bounded solve time, not accuracy.

**The mechanical lane** is a LiDAR mast: a 6061-T6 tube, 100 mm long, 20 mm outside diameter, 1.5 mm
wall, carrying a 0.175 kg sensor at the tip. Hand calculation, CalculiX FEA, a mesh-convergence study, a
sightline tolerance stack, and a frozen protocol for a static compliance test that has **not been run**.
The mast was redesigned once because the first geometry failed a modal guard.

**What connects them** is a discipline, not a technology: a number's value depends on where it came
from. The repo enforces that with fail-closed checkers — a release refuses while any input is pending, a
verdict refuses while any required condition is unevaluated, and a calibration cannot be called usable
while its own adequacy check fails.

## The three things you should be proudest of

1. **You found and fixed errors in your own work, in public.** The claim ledger records claims that were
   withdrawn — including an attribution of a frequency gap to mechanisms that turned out not to be in
   the model at all. Most portfolios hide that. Yours is the evidence you can do engineering.
2. **The checkers say no.** Fail-closed gates that refuse on missing inputs are worth more than any
   result, because they are why the results you do have can be trusted.
3. **The limits are written next to the claims**, not in a footnote. "Simulation output", "model
   assumption", "not device-tested" appear throughout.

## The three things you must be ready to own

Read [02](02-interview-defence.md) for how to handle each. In short:

1. **100 % VAF is a tautology here**, not a triumph. Say so before anyone asks.
2. **Pure pursuit beating MPC has no supporting citation** and one credible contradicting result on real
   F1TENTH hardware. It is scoped to your plant and speed regime.
3. **Nothing mechanical has been measured.** The mast package is a coherent design study on assumed
   inputs, and the honest framing is that the experimental half is unbuilt.
